:page-faq-1-q: How long do startup CTOs usually stay in the role?
:page-faq-1-a: Usually a few years. Korn Ferry found that CIO and CTO average tenure at the top 1,000 US companies was about 4.3 years, against 6.9 for CEOs, and AKF Partners puts US CTO tenures at three to 5.5 years. No rigorous study tracks founding CTOs specifically; the nearest, Noam Wasserman's study of more than 10,000 founders, found that 52% of founder-CEOs had been replaced by the third financing round. Chapters 10 and 16 of Applied Alchemy cover the evidence and what it means for a CTO deciding whether to stay.
:page-faq-2-q: How much time does a startup CTO spend coding?
:page-faq-2-a: It depends on the size of the company. Daniel Jarjoura's model puts coding at 70% or more of a founding CTO's time up to about 15 employees, 10 to 40% at 15 to 45, under 10% at 45 to 250, and none beyond that. AI tools are pulling some leaders back towards the code: LeadDev's 2026 survey found 44% of CTOs spending more time on hands-on coding and code review than a year earlier. Chapter 1 of Applied Alchemy sets out the four stages.
:page-faq-3-q: How common is burnout among startup CTOs?
:page-faq-3-a: Common, and getting worse. LeadDev's Engineering Leadership Report 2026 found that 54% of CTOs felt emotionally drained by their work at least once a week, up from 24% a year earlier, although CTOs were only 6% of the people surveyed. A CTO Craft survey of 100 tech leaders found that 97% had felt lonely as a leader. Chapter 15 of Applied Alchemy treats both as structural features of the role rather than personal failings.
:page-faq-4-q: Why do startup CTOs get fired?
:page-faq-4-a: Rarely for their technical skills. CTO adviser Adelina Chalmers reports that of the founding CTOs she has seen fired, 70% could not communicate with the board and other non-technical leaders, 30% were not strategic enough, and none were fired for their technical skills. AKF Partners names a failure to build trust with the rest of the executive team as the most frequent reason. Chapter 10 of Applied Alchemy argues that the business acumen gap, not code, is what costs CTOs their jobs.
:page-faq-5-q: Does AI make software teams more productive?
:page-faq-5-a: It produces more code, but not yet proportionally more delivered work. In Fiona Chen and James Stratton's study of 655 firms, adopting AI coding agents brought 35.7% more pull requests but only 13.7% more resolved issues, and the time from review request to approval rose 56.1%. METR's 2026 follow-up trial estimated speedups of 4 to 18% but called its own data only very weak evidence. Chapters 6 and 12 of Applied Alchemy explain why review capacity, not typing speed, is now the constraint.
= By the Numbers

*In short:* In LeadDev's 2026 survey, 54% of CTOs felt emotionally drained at least weekly, up from 24% a year earlier. A study of 655 firms found that AI coding agents raised pull requests by 35.7% but resolved issues by only 13.7%. And no rigorous study tracks founding CTO replacement; the nearest finds 52% of founder-CEOs replaced by the third round.

== What These Numbers Can and Can't Tell You

Most of these figures come from surveys, and a survey is only as good as the people who answer it. CTOs were 6% of LeadDev's respondents, so its CTO figures rest on a small subsample. The best-known replacement figure tracks founder-CEOs, not CTOs, because no equivalent study of CTOs exists. The reasons CTOs are fired come from one adviser's practice. Several AI figures come from companies that sell engineering analytics, and the largest study of AI and delivery is a working paper that has not yet been peer-reviewed.

The AI figures also date fastest. METR's 2025 trial, which found experienced developers slower with AI, was out of date within a year, and Veracode's security pass rate moved within months. Treat anything about AI as a reading taken in 2026, not a constant.

What the figures can tell you is direction. CTOs are under more strain than a year ago, the role changes faster than the people in it, and AI has moved the bottleneck in software from writing code to reviewing it. Each of those holds across more than one source on this page.

'''''

Numbers like these are the easiest part of the book to quote and the easiest to misread. Each comes from a single study with its own sample, its own date and its own definitions. Follow the link, read the chapter, and check the source before you put a figure in front of your board.
